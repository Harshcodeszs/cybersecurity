# Module 1 : Intro to Cybersecurity
## What is Cybersecurity?
- Cybersecurity is the ongoing effort to protect individuals, organizations and governments from digital attacks by protecting networked systems and data from unauthorized use or harm.

- **Offline Identity Theft**: Stealing personal information from physical documents, such as bank statements or tax returns.
- **Online Identity Theft**: Stealing personal information from online sources, such as social media accounts or email accounts.

## Types of Orgnizational Data
- **Traditional Data**: Data that is stored in physical form, such as paper documents or physical records.
- **Transactional Data**: Data that is generated as a result of business transactions, such as sales records or financial statements.
- **Intellectual Property**: Data that is created by an organization, such as patents, trademarks, or copyrights.
- **Financial Data**: Data that is related to an organization's financial performance, such as revenue, expenses, or profits.
- **Internet of Things (IoT) and Big Data**: Data that is generated by connected devices, such as sensors, cameras, or IoT devices.
- **The Cube**: 
  - **Confidentiality**: Ensuring that data is only accessible to authorized individuals or systems.
  - **Integrity**: Ensuring that data is accurate and complete, and has not been tampered with or altered.
  - **Availability**: Ensuring that data is accessible and usable when needed.
 ### The Protection Of Information in each state
 - **Processing**: The collection, storage, and use of data.
 - **Storage**: The physical or digital storage of data.
 - **Transmission**: The transfer of data from one location to another.
 ### The Security Measures Used to Protect Data
- **Awareness, training and education**: Educating employees about cybersecurity best practices and potential threats.
- **Technology**: Implementing security measures such as firewalls, antivirus software, and encryption to protect data.
- **Policies and procedures**: Establishing guidelines and protocols for handling data and responding to security incidents.
### Consequences of a Data Breach
- Reputational damage
- Vandalism
- Theft 
- Loss of revenue

- **Security Breach**: A security breach is an incident in which an unauthorized individual gains access to a system or network, potentially compromising sensitive data or causing damage to the system. Security breaches can occur through various means, such as hacking, phishing, malware, or social engineering.

## Types of Hackers
- **Amateur Hackers(Script Kiddies)**: Individuals who use pre-existing tools and scripts to exploit vulnerabilities in systems, often without a deep understanding of how the tools work.
- **Hackers**: Individuals who have a deep understanding of computer systems and networks, and use their knowledge to exploit vulnerabilities for personal gain or to cause harm.
- **White Hat Hackers**: Ethical hackers who use their skills to identify and fix vulnerabilities in systems, often working for organizations to improve their security.
- **Black Hat Hackers**: Hackers who use their skills to cause harm to systems, often for personal gain or to cause harm to organizations.
- **Grey Hat Hackers**: Hackers who may violate ethical or legal standards, but do not have malicious intent. They may exploit vulnerabilities to raise awareness or to demonstrate their skills.
- **Organized Hackers**: Groups of hackers who work together to exploit vulnerabilities in systems, often for financial gain or to cause harm to organizations.

## Types of threats
- Internal Threats: Threats that originate from within an organization, such as employees or contractors who have access to sensitive data and systems.
- External Threats: Threats that originate from outside an organization, such as cybercriminals or nation-states who may use their resources to attack an organization's systems.

# Cyberwarfare
- Cyberwarfare is the use of digital attacks by one nation-state to disrupt the computer systems of another nation-state, often with the goal of causing damage or gaining a strategic advantage. Cyberwarfare can take many forms, including hacking, malware, denial-of-service attacks, and other forms of cyber espionage. It is often used as a tool of modern warfare, and can have significant impacts on national security, economic stability, and public safety.

- stuxnet - Self-replicating computer worm that was designed to target and disrupt industrial control systems, specifically those used in nuclear facilities. # Module 2: Attack, Concept, and Techniques
### Type of Malwares
- **Spyware**: is software secretly installed on your computer, phone, or tablet to spy on what you do without your knowledge or consent
- **Adware**: is software that automatically generates or displays unwanted advertisements on your screen.
- **Backdoor**: is a method of bypassing normal authentication or encryption in a computer system, product, or embedded device, to gain access to the system or data.
- **Ransomware**: is a type of malicious software that encrypts a victim's files and demands payment (usually in cryptocurrency) in exchange for the decryption key.
- **Scareware**: is a type of malicious software that is designed to scare or intimidate users into taking a specific action, such as downloading additional software or providing personal information.
- **Rootkit**: is a type of malicious software that is designed to hide the presence of other malware on a system, often by modifying system files or processes.
- **Virus**: is a type of malicious software that is designed to replicate itself and spread to other computers, often by attaching itself to files or programs.
- **Trojan Horse**: is a type of malicious software that is disguised as a legitimate program or file, but actually contains harmful code that can damage or compromise a system.
- **Worm**: is a type of malicious software that is designed to spread across networks and systems, often by exploiting vulnerabilities in software or operating systems. Unlike viruses, worms do not require user interaction to spread, and can cause significant damage to networks and systems.

### Social Engineering
- **Social engineering** is the manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. Social engineers often rely on people’s willingness to be helpful, but they also prey on their weaknesses. Hacking people instead of computers is often easier and more effective.
### Types of Social Engineering Attacks
- Pretexting: This is when an attacker calls an individual and lies to them in an attempt to gain access to privileged data.
- Tailgating: This is when an attacker follows an authorized person into a restricted area without proper authentication.
- Someone or something (quid pro quo): This is when an attacker offers a service or benefit in exchange for information or access.

### Denial of Service 
- Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks are a type of network attack that is relatively simple to carry out, even by an unskilled attacker. A DoS attack results in some sort of interruption of network service to users, devices or applications.

### Two main types of DoS attacks
- overwhelming the target with traffic, which can be done by sending a large number of requests to a server or network device, causing it to become overloaded and unable to respond to legitimate requests.
- Malicously formating packets, which can exploit vulnerabilities in network protocols or software to cause a system to crash or become unresponsive.

- **DDoS** Distributed Denial-of-Service attacks are a type of DoS attack that involves multiple systems working together to overwhelm a target with traffic or requests. DDoS attacks are often carried out using botnets, which are networks of compromised computers or devices that can be controlled remotely.   
- **Botnet**:A botnet (short for robot network) is a network of internet-connected devices—like computers, smartphones, routers, or smart TVs—that have been infected with malware and are controlled remotely by a hacker without the owners' knowledge.
- **On-path attacks**: An on-path attack, also known as a man-in-the-middle (MITM) attack, On-path attackers intercept or modify communications between two devices, such as a web browser and a web server, either to collect information from or to impersonate one of the devices.
- Man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks are a type of on-path attack where an attacker intercepts and alters communications between two parties without their knowledge. The attacker can eavesdrop on the communication, steal sensitive information, or impersonate one of the parties to gain access to resources or data.
- Man in mobile (MITM) attacks are a type of on-path attack that specifically targets mobile devices, such as smartphones or tablets. In a MITM attack, an attacker intercepts and alters communications between a mobile device and a server or application, often by exploiting vulnerabilities in the mobile operating system or applications. The attacker can steal sensitive information, such as login credentials or financial data, or impersonate the user to gain access to resources or data.
- **Seo poisoning**:  is about improving an organization’s website so that it gains greater visibility in search engine results. malicious website shows up at the very top of Google search results when you look for something legitimate.
- **Wifi Password cracking**: is the process of attempting to gain unauthorized access to a wireless network by guessing or cracking the password used to secure the network. This can be done using various techniques, such as brute force attacks, dictionary attacks, or exploiting vulnerabilities in the wireless protocol.


### Password Attacks
- Password Spraying: is a technique used by attackers to gain unauthorized access to multiple accounts by using a single password or a small set of passwords across different accounts. This is possible because many people reuse passwords across multiple accounts, making it easier for attackers to compromise multiple accounts with a single password.
- Dictionary Attack: A hacker systematically tries every word in a dictionary or a list of commonly used words as a password in an attempt to break into a password-protected account.
- Rainbow Attack: A rainbow attack is a type of password attack that uses precomputed tables of hashed passwords to quickly crack password hashes. The attacker can use these tables to look up the hash of a password and find the corresponding plaintext password, allowing them to gain unauthorized access to accounts or systems. you should use salt to make it more difficult for attackers to use rainbow tables to crack password hashes.
- Traffic Inception: Plain text or unencrypted passwords can be easily read by other humans and machines by intercepting communications.

### Advanced Persistent Threats (APT)
- An advanced persistent threat (APT) is a prolonged and targeted cyberattack in which an intruder gains unauthorized access to a network and remains undetected for an extended period of time. APTs are typically carried out by highly skilled and well-funded attackers, often with the goal of stealing sensitive information or intellectual property, disrupting operations, or gaining a strategic advantage. APTs can be difficult to detect and mitigate, as they often involve sophisticated techniques such as social engineering, malware, and zero-day exploits. Organizations can protect against APTs by implementing strong security measures, such as network segmentation, intrusion detection and prevention systems, and employee training on cybersecurity best practices.


# Security Vulnerabilities
- A security vulnerability is a weakness or flaw in a system, application, or network that can be exploited by an attacker to gain unauthorized access, steal data, or cause damage. Vulnerabilities can arise from various sources, such as software bugs, misconfigurations, or design flaws. 

# Hardware Vulnerabilities
- Hardware vulnerabilities are security weaknesses or flaws that exist in the physical components of a computer system or device, such as the CPU, memory, or storage. These vulnerabilities can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access to sensitive data, execute malicious code, or disrupt system operations.   

# Software Vulnerabilities
- Software vulnerabilities are security weaknesses or flaws that exist in software applications or operating systems. These vulnerabilities can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access to systems, steal data, or execute malicious code.

- **Buffer Overflow**: A buffer overflow is a type of software vulnerability that occurs when a program attempts to write more data to a buffer (a temporary storage area in memory) than it can hold. This can cause the program to crash or allow an attacker to execute arbitrary code, potentially leading to unauthorized access or control of the system.
- **Non-validated Input**: Non-validated input is a type of software vulnerability that occurs when a program does not properly validate user input, allowing an attacker to submit malicious data that can be used to exploit the system. This can lead to various types of attacks, such as SQL injection, cross-site scripting (XSS), or command injection.
- **Race Conditions**: A race condition is a type of software vulnerability that occurs when two or more processes or threads access shared resources in an unpredictable order, leading to unexpected behavior or errors. This can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access, execute arbitrary code, or cause system crashes.
- **Weakness in security practices**: Weaknesses in security practices can lead to software vulnerabilities, such as using weak passwords, failing to apply security patches, or not properly configuring security settings. These weaknesses can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access or compromise the system.
- **Access control  problems**: Access control problems occur when a system does not properly enforce access controls, allowing unauthorized users to access sensitive data or perform actions they should not be able to. This can lead to various types of attacks, such as privilege escalation or data breaches.

# Firewall
- **Network Firewall** - This filter communication  based on the source and destination IP address, port number, and protocol type. It can be hardware-based, software-based, or a combination of both, and is used to protect networks and devices from unauthorized access, malware, and other security threats.
- **Transport Layer Firewall** - This filter communication based on the source and destination IP address, port number, and protocol type. It can be hardware-based, software-based, or a combination of both, and is used to protect networks and devices from unauthorized access, malware, and other security threats.
- **Application Layer Firewall** - This filter communication based on the application or service being used, such as web browsing or email. It can be hardware-based, software-based, or a combination of both, and is used to protect networks and devices from unauthorized access, malware, and other security threats.
- **Context-Aware Firewall** - This filter communication based on the context of the traffic, such as the user, device, or location. It can be hardware-based, software-based, or a combination of both, and is used to protect networks and devices from unauthorized access, malware, and other security threats.
- **Proxy server** - This is a server that acts as an intermediary between a user's device and the internet. It can be used to filter traffic, block malicious content, and provide anonymity for users. Proxy servers can also be used to cache frequently accessed content, improving performance and reducing bandwidth usage.
- **Reverse Proxy Server** - This is a server that sits between a user's device and a web server, forwarding requests from the user to the web server and returning responses back to the user. Reverse proxy servers can be used to improve performance, provide load balancing, and enhance security by hiding the identity of the web server and filtering traffic.
- **Network Address Translation (NAT)** - This is a technique used to map private IP addresses to public IP addresses, allowing multiple devices on a local network to share a single public IP address. NAT can help improve security by hiding the internal network structure and preventing direct access to devices from the internet.
- **Host-based Firewall** - This is a firewall that is installed on an individual device, such as a computer or server, to monitor and control incoming and outgoing network traffic. Host-based firewalls can provide an additional layer of security by filtering traffic at the device level, and can be configured to allow or block specific applications or services.

# Detection and Prevention Systems
- **Intrusion Detection System** (IDS): An intrusion detection system (IDS) is a network security device that monitors network traffic for signs of malicious activity or policy violations. It can detect and alert on suspicious traffic, but does not take action to prevent attacks. IDS can be used to identify potential threats and provide detailed logging and reporting for analysis and investigation.
- **Intrusion Prevention System** (IPS): An intrusion prevention system (IPS) is a network security device that monitors network traffic for signs of malicious activity or policy violations. It can detect and prevent attacks in real-time by blocking or alerting on suspicious traffic, and can also provide detailed logging and reporting for analysis and investigation. IPS can be used to identify and prevent potential threats, and can be configured to automatically block or quarantine malicious traffic.

# Security Best Practices
- **Perform a risk assesment** - Knowing and understanding the value of what you are protecting will help to justify security expenditures.
- **Create a security policy** - Create a policy that clearly outlines the organization’s rules, job roles, and responsibilities and expectations for employees.
- **Physical security measures** - Restrict access to networking closets and server locations, as well as fire suppression.
- **Human resources security measures** - Background checks should be completed for all employees.
- **Perform and test backups** - Back up information regularly and test data recovery from backups.
- **Maintain security patches and updates** - Regularly update server, client and network device operating systems and programs.
- **Employ access controls** - Configure user roles and privilege levels as well as strong user authentication.
- **Regularly test incident response** - Employ an incident response team and test emergency response scenarios.
- **Implement a network monitoring, analytics and management tool** - Choose a security monitoring solution that integrates with other technologies.
- **Implement network security devices** - Use next generation routers, firewalls and other security appliances.
- **Implement a comprehensive endpoint security solution** - Use enterprise level antimalware and antivirus software.
- **Educate users** - Provide training to employees in security procedures.
One of the most widely known and respected organizations for cybersecurity training is the SANS Institute. 
- **Encrypt data** - Encrypt all sensitive organizational data, including email.

# Behavior Based Security
- Honeypots: A honeypot is a security mechanism that is designed to attract and trap potential attackers, allowing security professionals to monitor and analyze their behavior. Honeypots can be used to gather intelligence on attack techniques, identify vulnerabilities, and improve overall security posture. They can be implemented as physical or virtual systems, and can be configured to mimic real systems or networks to lure attackers into revealing their tactics and methods.

- Netflow: NetFlow is a network protocol developed by Cisco Systems that collects and analyzes network traffic data. It provides information about the source and destination of network traffic, as well as the type of traffic and the amount of data being transmitted. NetFlow can be used for network monitoring, performance analysis, and security analysis, allowing organizations to identify potential threats and optimize network performance.

# Penetration Testing
- **Step 1: Planning** - The pen tester gathers as much information as possible about a target system or network, its potential vulnerabilities and exploits to use against it. This involves conducting passive or active reconnaissance (footprinting) and vulnerability research.
- **Step 2: Scanning** - The pen tester carries out active reconnaissance to probe a target system or network and identify potential weaknesses which, if exploited, could give an attacker access. Active reconnaissance may include:
  - Port scanning to identify open ports and services
  - vulnerability scanning to identify potential exploitable vulnerabilities of a particular target
  - establishing an active connection to a target (enumeration) to identify the user account, system account and admin account.
- **Step 3: Gaining Access** - The pen tester will attempt to gain access to a target system and sniff network traffic, using various methods to exploit the system including:
  - launching an exploit with a payload onto the system
  - breaching physical barriers to assets
  - social engineering
  - exploiting website vulnerabilities
  - exploiting software and hardware vulnerabilities or misconfigurations
  - breaching access controls security
  - cracking weak encrypted Wi-Fi.
- **Step 4: Maintaining Access** - The pen tester will maintain access to the target to find out what data and systems are vulnerable to exploitation. It is important that they remain undetected, typically using backdoors, Trojan horses, rootkits and other covert channels to hide their presence.

When this infrastructure is in place, the pen tester will then proceed to gather the data that they consider valuable.
- **Step 5: Analysis and Reporting** - The pen tester will provide feedback via a report that recommends updates to products, policies and training to improve an organization’s security.**

# Impact Reduction
- **Communicate the issue** - Communication creates transparency, which is critical in this type of situation.
Internally, all employees should be informed and a clear call to action communicated.
Externally, all clients should be informed through direct communication and official announcements.
- **Be sincere and accountable** - Respond to the breach in an honest and genuine way, taking responsibility where the organization is at fault.
- **Provide the details** - Be open and explain why the breach took place and what information was compromised. Organizations are generally expected to take care of any client costs associated with identity theft services required as a result of a security breach.
- **Find the cause** - Take steps to understand what caused and facilitated the breach. This may involve hiring forensics experts to research and find out the details.
- **Apply lessons learned** - Make sure that any lessons learned from forensic investigations are applied to prevent similar breaches from happening in the future.
- **Check, and check again** - Attackers will often attempt to leave a backdoor to facilitate future breaches. To prevent this from happening, make sure that all systems are clean, no backdoors are installed and nothing else has been compromised.
- **Educate!** - Raise awareness, train and educate employees, partners and clients on how to prevent future breaches.

# Risk Management
- **Frame the risk** - Identify the threats that increase risk. Threats may
include processes, products, attacks, potential failure or
disruption of services, negative perception of an
organization's reputation, potential legal liability or loss
of intellectual property.
- **Access the risk** -  Determine the severity that each threat poses. For
example, some threats may have the potential to bring
an entire organization to a standstill, while other threats
may be only minor inconveniences. Risk can be
prioritized by assessing financial impact (a quantitative
analysis) or scaled impact on an organization's operation
(a qualitative analysis).
- **Respond the risk** - Develop an action plan to reduce overall organization
risk exposure, detailing where risk can be eliminated,
mitigated, transferred or accepted.
- **Monitor the risk** - Continuously review any risk reduced through
elimination, mitigation or transfer actions. Remember,
not all risks can be eliminated, so you will need to
closely monitor any threats that have been accepted.

# Secuirity Playbook 
-  A security playbook is a collection of repeatable queries or reports that outline a standardized process for incident detection
and response. Ideally, a security playbook should:
- highlight how to identify and automate the response to common threats such as the detection of malware-infected

- provide usable and quick access to key statistics and metrics.

# Tools for Incident Detection and Prevention
- **A Security Information and Event Management** (SIEM)
system collects and analyzes security alerts, logs and
other real-time and historical data from security devices
on the network to facilitate early detection of cyber
attacks.

- **A Data Loss Prevention** (DLP) system is designed to
stop sensitive data from being stolen from or escaping a
network. It monitors and protects data in three different
states: data in use (data being accessed by a user), data
in motion (data traveling through the network) and data
at rest (data stored in a computer network or device).
